How it works

  • Type or paste text into the box.
  • Counts update instantly with every keystroke.
  • Reading time is estimated at 200 words per minute, a common average for adult silent reading.

Common uses

  • Checking whether a social media post or bio fits a character limit.
  • Getting a rough silent-reading estimate for a draft; spoken delivery and technical reading can take longer.
  • Tracking word count progress while drafting an essay or article.
Your privacy matters here: check the FAQ below for exactly what happens to anything you enter into this tool.

Understand the count

Words are counted by whitespace. Hyphenated words and text without spaces may differ from counts in a word processor. Reading time is an estimate, not a measure of comprehension.

A practical walkthrough

  1. Paste a draft of an article or essay, then watch its word and character totals as you shorten the text.
  2. Compare the final count with the submission platform, especially when punctuation or languages without spaces are involved.
  3. Read the totals above as you edit. Select and copy your source text manually if needed; there is no report download.

Tips for a reliable result

Reading time is an estimate. A technical article usually takes longer to understand than a simple passage of the same length.

Questions and troubleshooting

Is my text saved or sent anywhere?

No. All counting happens locally in your browser as you type; nothing is transmitted or stored.

How is reading time calculated?

Word count divided by 200 words per minute, rounded up to the nearest minute.

Does it count numbers and punctuation as words?

Numbers are counted as words if they're separated by spaces. Punctuation attached to a word doesn't count separately.

When a count differs from another app

Compare the exact final text, including whitespace and line breaks. This counter uses whitespace for word boundaries and JavaScript string length for characters, so emoji and some languages can differ from another editor. The receiving form's counting rule determines its submission limit.

Why does another editor show a different count?

Word boundaries vary between applications, particularly for punctuation, hyphenated words, and languages without spaces. Use the destination platform's count when a submission has a strict limit.

Are character limits the same everywhere?

No. Platforms can count line breaks, spaces, emoji, and Unicode characters differently. Check the final text in the place where it will be submitted.
