Choose a photo with a clear subject

A background-removal model estimates which pixels belong to the subject. It works from visual evidence in the image, so the source photograph strongly affects the result. Start with a sharp photo, useful contrast between the subject and its surroundings, and enough room around the edges. A white product against a white wall gives the model fewer visible boundaries than the same product against a contrasting background.

For product photography, use even lighting and avoid a background that shares the product's colors or texture. Keep important handles, straps, and thin parts visible. Retaking a photo can be more effective than repeatedly processing a difficult image. The tool has no manual brush for restoring missed pixels.

Why hair and transparent materials are difficult

Hair, fur, mesh, and fine branches contain many small transitions between subject and background. Motion blur and poor focus blend those transitions. Inspect these areas at full size after processing, especially if the cutout will appear against a very different background. An edge that looks acceptable on white can show a pale halo on black.

Glass, smoke, and semi-transparent fabric are difficult for another reason: part of the original background is visible through the subject. Removing the surroundings does not necessarily reconstruct what a transparent object would look like on an entirely new scene. Reflective surfaces may also retain colors from the original environment. Treat these outputs as starting points for an image-editing workflow when accuracy matters.

Run a manageable local test

  1. Open Background Remover and choose a supported image.
  2. Allow the browser to load the required runtime and model resources.
  3. Wait for the progress or status message; the first run may take longer.
  4. Download the PNG and open that file in an image viewer.
  5. Inspect its outline on both a light and a dark background.

The image processing happens in your browser. Loading the application and its model resources still uses the network. Large images and other open tabs can increase memory pressure, particularly on a phone. If a run fails, try a smaller working copy while keeping the original image. Avoid closing or reloading the tab while it is processing.

Transparency is different from a white background

A transparent PNG includes information about pixel opacity. A white rectangle surrounding a subject is simply visible white image content. Many viewers show transparency as a checkerboard, while others display it over white. Place the result on a colored background to distinguish the two.

Keep the transparent output as PNG for a workflow that needs transparency. Converting it to JPEG removes the transparency channel and requires an opaque background. A later conversion back to PNG does not restore the removed transparency. Keep the original cutout so you can export different versions without starting over.

Check the actual destination

For a product listing, inspect the required dimensions, background rules, and crop. For a design, check the final size and background color rather than only a large preview. Removing a background does not increase the detail in a small source, and enlarging the cutout may make edge defects more visible.

For images on a website, describe meaningful visual information in suitable alternative text. Decorative images may need different handling. The W3C image decision tree can help decide how an image functions in its final context.
